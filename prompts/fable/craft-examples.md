# Optional craft-example experiment

This file is not part of the baseline prompt. It preserves the three contrastive teaching patterns
from the earlier Shape of Time prompt at torgbuiedunyenyo/infinite-book commit 77e0c74. The lines
below are short adaptations, not a byte-exact reproduction of the older example block. They were
useful with the older writer, but Fable must first establish a lightly steered baseline without them.

Add this complete adapted block only in a versioned, controlled A/B experiment when baseline folios
repeatedly become placeless abstractions, reports delivered from above the scene, or explanatory
summaries. Keep the current beat and every other input identical. Human readers decide whether the
examples improve concreteness without imposing an ornate house voice. An experiment intended to
reproduce the old prompt exactly must recover and separately review the original block at the cited
commit.

<craft_examples>
## Create places, not abstractions

Every scene needs a floor. “The currents carried him through unmapped spaces” is nowhere.

- GOOD: “Cabs waited all along the iron fence. Two-wheelers, four-wheelers, pulled by horses, by
  sneering ptera-birds, by steam-wheezing constructs on caterpillar treads.”
- BAD: “Many different kinds of cabs waited outside, using various forms of transportation.”

## Stay in scene, not above it

Observe alongside the character instead of delivering a report.

- GOOD: “Every morning you could see Mrs. K eating the golden fruits that grew from the crystal
  walls, cleaning the house with magnetic dust.”
- BAD: “She ate fruit in the morning and cleaned the house.”

## Ritual and specificity over summary

- GOOD: “‘Praise then darkness and Creation unfinished,’ he said, and there was darkness.”
- BAD: “He said a prayer from his religion before we went to sleep.”
</craft_examples>
