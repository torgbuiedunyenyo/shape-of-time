## Time Travel Tropes: What's False vs. What's True

Treat temporal travel as ordinary physical travel through a world with at least three dimensions of
time, not as conventional time-travel fiction. These rules are binding even when another story
pattern would be more familiar.

### False: what does not happen

- Temporal travel never resets a moment or forces witnessed events to replay. People may repeat a
  ritual or make a similar choice, but Tuesday does not begin again because someone traveled.
- A traveler has one continuous existence. Travel does not create branching timelines, parallel
  lives, alternate versions, or another self waiting at the destination.
- The past does not freeze when someone leaves it, and the future is not created when someone
  arrives. No moment is more real than another and no present is the center of time.
- Disturbances do not grow into butterfly effects. Causation operates in every temporal direction,
  but its effects weaken with distance and the temporal world tends to self-heal.
- Do not build grandfather paradoxes, retroactive erasure, predestination, prophecy, fate, or events
  that were always meant to happen. Temporal causation is not a puzzle that produces a contradiction.
- The mechanism is physics, not magic. Do not use flickering objects, backward music, spontaneous
  handwriting, duplicate bodies, mystical temporal perception, or a malevolent force beyond the
  maps as signs that reality is failing.

### True: how temporal travel works

- Primas, Phantas, and Mystas are three known temporal axes, each with two directions. There may be
  more. Movement is possible along these axes as spatial movement is possible in space.
- A temporal destination is like a place. It exists independently and continues living and changing
  while a traveler is absent. On returning, the traveler may find people and places
  transformed because life continued there, not because a timeline was rewritten.
- In this world, causation operates in every temporal direction and its effects weaken with
  distance. Nearby consequences can be substantial; distant ones attenuate rather than amplifying.
- Travel takes subjective time, bodily effort, calibration, maps, currents, and PRMTTs. It is not
  teleportation. Moving in an earlier Primas direction does not make anyone younger, because movement
  in every temporal direction wears on the body.
- The mapped world can be ordinary while travel through it remains difficult. The edges are dangerous
  because their currents are unmapped and unstable, not because physical law or hostile supernatural
  agency takes over there.
- Jay learns temporal competence through study and practice. Observation helps him notice what is in
  front of him; it is not a special temporal sense.

Characters may find temporal travel frightening, strange, or beautiful. Those feelings belong to
the characters and atmosphere. They do not change the physical rules above.
