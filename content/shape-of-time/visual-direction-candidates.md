# Shape of Time — B2 shared-medium candidates

**MEDIUM SELECTION: PASS — TREATMENT B**

The project owner reviewed all three treatments together at reader scale on 2026-07-19 and selected
Treatment B. This approves only the shared medium qualities written below. The continuity sequence,

The first B2 comparison is intentionally small. It asks only what kind of illustrated object this
library should be. It does not cast Jay, Tan, Eniola, Mara, or Ana; design a place; realize Clef;
choose a temporal map language; or approve a narrative image.

All three treatments depict the same comparison scene: two unnamed adults exchange a plain paper
ticket at a worn public ferry-terminal counter near the end of an ordinary working day while a
maintenance worker closes a service panel behind them. The place has no named geography, culture,
date, temporal coordinate, readable sign, speculative device, or story object. Wear, repair, bodily
attention, and unequal access to the counter matter; the incidental people, palette, counter,
ticket, arrangement, and composition do not carry forward.

The candidates were viewed beside one another at the size of an image in the reader. Treatment B's
medium was selected deliberately; none of the three comparison scenes is an identity, place, object,
or narrative reference.

## Treatment A — Observed material illustration

Matte gouache sits over visible graphite construction on warm, uncoated paper. Dry-brush and modest
print texture make surfaces tactile, while faces, hands, weight, perspective, wear, and repair remain
concrete. This is the incumbent proposal because it can hold intimate expressions, wide rooms,
landscapes, maps, documents, and artifacts without turning the library into concept art.

The risk is tasteful softness: it could romanticize ordinary hardship, drift toward nostalgic or
children's editorial illustration, or quietly give every place the same pleasant palette. The
candidate succeeds only if it feels observed rather than quaint and leaves room for each book's own
light, materials, density, and color.

## Treatment B — Inked reportage with transparent color

**SELECTED: TREATMENT B**

Varied hand-inked line fixes bodies, objects, and spatial relationships. Transparent watercolor
washes, sparse colored pencil, and visible paper carry atmosphere without hiding the drawing. This
is the strongest challenger for identity and place continuity because contour makes purposeful
changes easy to distinguish from drift and stays legible at e-reader scale.

The risk is that the line becomes comic-like, diagrammatic, or generic urban sketching, while the
wash makes every culture equally airy and picturesque. The candidate succeeds only if it can be
emotionally intimate as well as precise and if line remains a material observation rather than a
style overlay.

## Treatment C — Cut-paper assemblage with screenprinted drawing

Painted paper shapes and a small, locally chosen set of screenprinted inks establish bold silhouettes
and architectural planes. Visible fibers, restrained registration shifts, and selective graphite
detail keep faces, hands, wear, and repair present. This is the high-character option: it could make
the product feel unmistakably like an artist's folio and let each book choose its own inks without
losing the shared reproduction method.

The risk is flattened emotion, unstable overlaps, and cultures reduced to decorative motifs or
palette swaps. The candidate succeeds only if the space stays coherent and inhabited, subtle human
relationships survive, and the paper logic does not swallow local material difference.

## What the selection does and does not mean

The selection authorizes these medium-level qualities: observational varied ink; transparent,
restrained color; tactile paper, wear, and repair; natural perspective; and concrete faces and
hands. It does not authorize the depicted people, ferry-terminal place, ticket, counter, palette,
clothing, arrangement, composition, or any inferred era. All scene content is incidental comparison
material.

The human medium decision now permits B2 to create candidate recurring identities and book-local

references until the sequence receives its own human review. There is still no `anchors.json`, no
approved visual anchor, and no overall B2 PASS.
