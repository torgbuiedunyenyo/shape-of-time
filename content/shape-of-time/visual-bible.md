# Shape of Time — Visual Development Proposal

**REVIEW_STATUS: PENDING_OWNER_REVIEW**

This is a proposal for the story-wide visual grammar, not approved visual authority and not a scene
plan. It is never an input to the Fable prose prompt. It does not decide Jay or Tan's face, the form
of Clef, the plan of a shop, the look of a PRMTT, or the staging of a future folio. Those decisions
are made only at the scope where they are needed: a recurring identity reference, a book-local visual
profile, or a folio image brief.

A generated image is a candidate. Once an image is deliberately approved and exposed, its relevant
details bind the book or lineage named by its production record; they do not silently become facts
about every book in the library.

## Proposed shared direction

The working direction is **observed material illustration**: concrete people, rooms, streets,
landscapes, tools, products, maps, and documents rendered with the tactility of matte gouache,
graphite construction, and restrained print texture. This shared medium should make the library feel
like one illustrated object without making its many places and times look like one place.

This is deliberately location-neutral. Each book derives its palette, light, density, materials,
clothing logic, graphic language, and compositional habits from its own setting and viewpoint. There
is no universal Bay Area palette and no era receives a moral color. Faces and material evidence stay
legible. Factual labels, map legends, and document fields are typeset deterministically rather than
entrusted to generated lettering.

Images share narrative labor with prose. Prose owns action, causality, dialogue, thought,
motivation, and basic orientation. Images may reveal place, material evidence, atmosphere,
relationship, contradiction, spatial knowledge, or something deliberately left unspoken. An image
that only decorates or paraphrases the neighboring sentences should be removed.

## A visual world larger than one timeline

Oakland is an anchor, not a boundary. It grounds the beginning of the root story, but later root
movements and other books may emerge in any place or temporal region that the world permits. The
library should eventually contain many places and times, including settlements, routes, stations,
preservation zones, tourist economies, underground spaces, and ordinary homes that no planning
document has named in advance.

The words past and future are relational descriptions of travel along Primas, not two kinds of era
and not coordinates in their own right. Every time is the present to the people living there,
continues changing while unoccupied, and may already bear causal, economic, or cultural pressure
from several temporal directions. Phantas and Mystas must not become palette filters attached to an
otherwise ordinary one-axis corridor. When they matter, the local book must work out how their
geography, currents, access, and causal relationships become materially visible.

There is no single visual shorthand for “the future.” Cultures reached from a given book by travel
along Primas should not default to chrome, neon, white seamless rooms, blue holograms, flying traffic,
or a generic tower city. They are cultures separated by temporal coordinate and place. Their
technologies may be bodily, infrastructural, ambient, repaired, inherited, invisible to an expert, or
visible mainly through a visitor's inability to use them. Different Primas coordinates should have
genuinely different built environments, clothing, transit, interfaces, typography, maintenance, and
relationships to other times.

For every new place and temporal coordinate, the book-local visual profile should answer in ordinary
language:

- What is normal here from a resident's point of view?
- Which other times have altered the economy, ecology, architecture, language, or daily habits?
- Who can use maps, travel, medicine, communication, protected space, and reliable infrastructure?
- What wear, repair, ownership, absence, adaptation, or maintenance makes that access visible?
- What does the focal person notice, misunderstand, or take for granted?
- What local palette, materials, light, density, and composition belong to this book?

These questions make an unfamiliar coordinate legible without prebuilding a universal style for it.

## World-grounded visual constraints

The visual system must preserve the physics in world.md without deciding designs that the source
leaves open.

- A person has one continuous body. Temporal movement never creates visual duplicates, alternate
  selves, or parallel versions.
- A returned-to place has continued living. Meaningful changes come from elapsed life and causal
  pressure, not reality glitches or a decorative before-and-after filter.
- Temporal travel takes experienced duration, calibration, maps, currents, bodily effort, spatial
  movement, light, and heat. It is not a glowing portal or instantaneous dissolve.
- PRMTTs may differ by era, maker, wealth, repair history, and book. Their global continuity is
  functional, not one preselected silhouette.
- Temporal maps represent changing three-dimensional currents. Direction, Major and Minor movement,
  confidence, turbulence, revision, and unequal access must be distinguishable when relevant. Exact
  colors, glyphs, and line systems await actual visual comparison, and color is never the only carrier
  of meaning.
- The edges remain physical places with unreliable currents and knowledge. They are not supernatural
  voids, malicious presences, or permission for abstract cosmic imagery to replace material scenes.
- Power appears through access, infrastructure, property, extraction, preservation, migration,
  service, repair, and who must ask whom for help—not through one faction being assigned sinister
  lighting.

## Clef stays open

Clef remains visually indeterminate at world scope. The source establishes its unusual social and
economic position and the role of demand from wealthier Primas coordinates in its existence; it does
not establish whether Clef is a drink, food, drug, preparation, package, or something that fits none
of those categories.
This document must not decide its substance, shape, color, packaging, method of consumption, or
effect.

The movement or book plan may let the model realize Clef when a particular story needs to encounter
it. Once that realization appears in exposed prose or imagery, retain the relevant portrayal within
that book for as long as the same instance requires continuity. Different books may realize it differently.
A child depicting the exact visible instance from its parent should explicitly inherit
the relevant reference; another book with the same title or subject remains free to reinterpret it.
No local realization becomes global Clef canon.

## Where decisions belong

World scope contains only shared physical rules, source-defined social relationships, and the
candidate common medium. It does not contain scene layouts or a catalogue of objects.

Recurring identity scope contains only reference images deliberately approved for a person who must
remain recognizable. Clothing, grooming, injury, age, fatigue, and posture belong to a named
appearance period rather than the permanent identity record.

A book-local visual profile contains the particular place and temporal region, local material
baseline, temporal pressures, access regime, palette and form tendencies, recurring people and
places, locally realized open concepts, and the parent details this lineage inherits or deliberately
reinterprets. These choices bind that book, not its siblings and not the whole world.

A folio image brief contains the actual scene: the image's narrative job, new visual fact, prose
withheld for the image, people and place present, action or relationship, composition, required
references, intentional changes, and unknowns it must not resolve. Weather, outfit, damage, camera,
object placement, and scene-specific architecture belong here when the folio needs them.

Incidental nonrecurring texture remains incidental. Publication does not turn every bottle, sign,
bystander, or background building into a global fact.

## Reference-image continuity

Consistency comes primarily from ordered image references, not from translating every appearance
into prose. A normal request uses the smallest relevant pack, usually two to five inputs:

- a recurring identity or appearance-period reference;
- a book-local place or object reference when it is actually recurring;
- the preceding or causal narrative image;
- the parent image when a child must inherit something visible; or
- both neighboring images when repairing or inserting an image between them.

Each input has a persisted role and order. The request states what must remain, what must change, and
what may remain incidental. Rejected candidates never become references. Reference use is scoped:
an image from one sibling book cannot leak into another merely because their titles or subjects are
similar.

## Book-local visual profile

Before a generated book needs its first image, its short natural-prose visual profile records:

- the book's place and temporal position relative to its inhabitants;
- its ordinary material and social baseline;
- cross-temporal pressures and unequal forms of access;
- the local realization of any open subject the book must show;
- recurring identities, places, or objects already inherited from the founding passage;
- local palette, light, materials, density, and compositional tendencies;
- which parent references are inherited, which are reinterpreted, and why; and
- questions this book must leave unanswered.

This is a production aid, not a second story bible. It should be no longer than the decisions the
actual book needs.

## Folio image brief

Every planned narrative image records:

1. **Narrative job:** one concrete proposition the image contributes.
2. **New visual fact:** information not already carried by adjacent prose.
3. **Prose withheld:** what the writing intentionally leaves for the image.
4. **Scene:** place, temporal position, people, action, and relationship at this folio.
5. **References:** ordered identity, appearance, place, object, parent, and neighboring images.
6. **Must remain / must change:** continuity and purposeful development.
7. **Open questions:** facts the image must not settle.
8. **Incidental detail:** texture allowed to appear without wider authority.
9. **Accessible equivalent:** alt text that carries the image's narrative contribution.

## Continuity experiment before approval

B2 should compare two or three genuinely different treatments of the proposed shared direction,
then test an 8–12-image sequence rather than approving the first attractive image. The sequence
should include a recurring person across close and wide views, a purposeful appearance change, a
return to a changed place, two materially distinct cultures at different Primas coordinates, a
non-Oakland location, a non-root central figure, meaningful Phantas or Mystas geography, a book-local
open object, a parent-to-child inheritance, and one neighbor repair.

Human review decides whether the sequence preserves identity and place, makes change intelligible,
distinguishes cultures at different coordinates without cliché, supports geographic and temporal
range, and gives each image narrative work. Approval should produce a small set of actual reference
assets and local profiles, not restore the deleted catalogue of speculative scene details.
