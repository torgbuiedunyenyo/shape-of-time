# Shape of Time — Finite Narrative Arc

story authority.

This document separates two kinds of material:

- **Source canon** restates the corrected world document without resolving its genuine unknowns.
- **A0 authored connective decisions** select the prototype movement's dramatic order and stopping
  point. They are compatible with source canon but remain proposals until the project owner approves
  them.

The six parts below describe one finite novel. They are not six interchangeable premises, an endless
continuation, or a menu of actions for the reader.

## Complete six-part arc

### [ARC-1] Part One — Meeting and courtship in Oakland

**Source canon.** In future-saturated 2025 Oakland, Jay works in a liquor or convenience shop whose
livelihood depends on future visitors. He knows their practical habits even though he cannot read a
temporal map or operate a PRMTT. When Tan enters, he recognizes her as a future person from the
aggregate of wrong notes rather than from a revelation. During the **phone and payment incident**,
her inability to use the physical device exposes her to embarrassment; Jay gives her the Clef
instead of completing a transaction. His act combines kindness, curiosity, and attraction.
(world.md:L170-L178; world.md:L182-L188; world.md:L324-L331)

Tan returns to the shop, then meets Jay elsewhere, then enters more of his life. Friendship becomes
**courtship** against the visible wealth difference between their eras. Jay understands the power
imbalance. Tan's care is genuine but incomplete: she can always leave, and she has not yet learned to
consider him as fully as she considers herself. The novel must preserve the source's asymmetry rather
than turn the pair into uncomplicated, equally situated lovers. (world.md:L188-L192;
world.md:L216-L220)

**Causal join:** The free Clef interrupts Tan's expected tourist transaction, which gives her a
reason to return; repeated contact lets attraction become a relationship; the relationship makes a
journey into Tan's world possible and dangerous.

### [ARC-2] Part Two — Arrival and dependence in the future

**Source canon.** Tan asks Jay to see her world. Her father uses corporate influence to arrange
documentation, housing, and a path toward legitimacy. His help can be loving and controlling at the
same time, but the source does not settle his private intent. Jay and Tan travel together to an
unspecified future era. (world.md:L194-L200; world.md:L222-L226)

There, Jay depends on Tan to interpret technology, space, and social cues. Her friends are polite but
condescending; her family is cold; strangers read him as a curiosity, a predator, or a man pursuing
citizenship and wealth. He studies temporal mechanics and looks for work, but adaptation is slow and
humiliating. The visa system and the temporal extraction economy turn the imbalance already present
in their romance into material dependency. (world.md:L107-L134; world.md:L226-L230)

**Causal join:** Jay's deliberate trust at the end of Part One puts him inside a system in which Tan
has mobility, fluency, family, and legal standing while he has borrowed permission. That dependence
makes her later absence immediately perilous for him.

### [ARC-3] Part Three — Tan disappears and Jay is blamed

**Source canon.** A few days after their arrival, Tan grows distracted, excited, and secretive about
the unmapped edges. One night she says she needs to check something and will return soon. She does not
say where she is going, and she does not return. (world.md:L232-L238)

Jay reports her missing. Within hours, authorities treat the past person, last seen with her and
without an alibi, as the obvious suspect. He is detained, questioned, released under surveillance,
and warned that charges are imminent. He runs. Tan's father's degree of knowledge and the company
security apparatus's purpose remain unresolved. (world.md:L202-L210; world.md:L240-L242)

**Causal join:** Tan's ability to leave without imagining Jay's exposure activates every legal and
social suspicion established in Part Two. Jay flees because remaining within the official process
will not produce a fair investigation.

### [ARC-4] Part Four — Flight becomes investigation

**Source canon.** Jay escapes into temporal margins and immigrant networks of guides, forgers,
fixers, and safe houses. He also reconstructs Tan's final days, learning about her father's company,
its research stations, the missing edge expeditions, and Tan's adventurous interest in the same
places. His route crosses past and future eras and eventually approaches the Mystas edges.
(world.md:L244-L255; world.md:L333-L342)

When Jay returns to 2025 Oakland, the place has continued changing in his absence and people do not
remember him quite as he remembers them. Time has not preserved a private original for him. Through
study and survival he becomes a capable time traveler, reversing his earlier formal illiteracy
without acquiring any supernatural faculty. (world.md:L17-L21; world.md:L252-L257)

**Causal join:** Flight gives Jay access to the routes and people excluded from Tan's corporate
world; investigation supplies a direction; practice transforms the dependent traveler into someone
able to reach the truth himself.

### [ARC-5] Part Five — The truth is ordinary privilege, not conspiracy

**Source canon.** Tan chose to travel toward the Mystas edges because she found them fascinating and
had the training, equipment, and access to treat the trip as an adventure. She expected to explain
when she returned “in her own time.” She was not kidnapped or silenced. It never occurred to her that
Jay would be blamed, hunted, and forced to flee; he was not real to her in the way she was real to
herself. (world.md:L259-L265)

Jay finds Tan safe and excited to describe her experience. The source permits either a reunion as she
returns or a meeting at a station near the edges; A0 does not choose between them. (world.md:L267)

**Causal join:** Jay's investigation resolves the external mystery, but its answer exposes the
relationship's foundational wound. Tan's omission was not a secret plot against him; it was the
consequence of the unequal regard already visible during their courtship.

### [ARC-6] Part Six — Forgiveness, separation, and return [TRUE-END]

**Source canon.** Jay has spent weeks or months frightened, exhausted, and learning to survive
across eras. He understands the company's operations and the future's exploitation of the past in a
way he cannot undo. He chooses to forgive Tan because holding the anger would cost him more, not
because her conduct was acceptable. (world.md:L269-L277)

Forgiveness does not restore the romance. Jay and Tan try briefly to continue, but staying together
would require Tan to see him fully, account for their power difference, and change how she moves
through the world. She will not do that work, and the relationship ends. (world.md:L279)

Jay returns alone to a changed but recognizable 2025 Oakland and resumes work in the shop, selling
Clef to future tourists. The edges remain unmapped, the extraction economy continues, and nothing is
systemically repaired. Jay nevertheless survives with knowledge and agency he did not have before:
he now knows time is larger than the single direction he was taught to see. This quiet return—not
romantic reconciliation, corporate defeat, or an expedition with Tan—is the novel's true ending.
(world.md:L281-L285)

## Pilot movement boundary [PILOT-BOUNDARY]

**A0 authored connective decision — PENDING project-owner review.** The finite prototype movement
ends after Jay deliberately accepts Tan's invitation to visit her world and before either of them
travels. This is the **decision threshold**. It closes the movement's question—whether Jay will trust
Tan enough to place himself inside her world—without compressing Parts Two through Six into a
synopsis or ending on an unanswered invitation. The later consequences remain part of the complete
novel above. Source support for the invitation and eventual journey is world.md:L222-L226.

The movement contains these dramatic phases, not yet a folio-by-folio beat map:

1. **Meeting:** Jay recognizes Tan in the shop through specific practical details.
2. The **phone and payment incident:** Tan's defensive embarrassment meets Jay's nontransactional
   gift of Clef.
3. **Courtship:** distinct return visits move from shop acquaintance to friendship and intimacy while
   keeping tourism, money, and Tan's power to leave perceptible.
4. **Motivated temporal orientation:** when Tan seriously proposes travel, Jay needs enough formal
   understanding to decide. A route or temporal map gives the conversation a purpose. Tan makes clear
   that Primas, Phantas, and Mystas are directions; other times continue evolving; and PRMTTs move
   through changing temporal currents rather than teleporting to fixed dates. Clef can ground mutual
   causation in an object already between them. Jay is learning a formal model for conditions he has
   lived with, not discovering that future tourists exist. (world.md:L11-L27; world.md:L44-L58;
   world.md:L291-L297)
5. Jay weighs affection against the real asymmetry: Tan can leave, while his travel will require her
   world, knowledge, and sponsorship. He gives an explicit answer and accepts. Travel, future arrival,
   and the first humiliation belong to the next movement.

This boundary creates a finite causal shape: a gift breaks the expected transaction; repeated
returns make a relationship; intelligible knowledge makes the stakes of travel real; Jay chooses.
The prototype may elaborate those joins in scenes, but it may not change the six-part outcome above
or imply that the decision resolves the relationship's inequality.

## Preserved unknowns

The pilot and later beat maps must not silently decide the future calendar year, the company name,
Clef's chemistry, effect, or route of consumption, Tan's father's private knowledge, the company
security apparatus's exact goal, or the Part Five reunion location. Clef's visible form, character
appearance, and recurring-location decisions belong to the reviewed visual bible, not to the first
generated image.
