# Reader-first editorial brief

This brief is for the bounded ten-folio reader proof. The complete world in
`content/shape-of-time/world.md` remains factual and story authority. The temporal rules remain
binding. This document only decides how the first movement is compressed for the prototype; it is
not another story bible and it does not summarize the later Shape of Time arc.

Titles are plain scene names. Keep each folio between 120 and 250 words, usually in two paragraphs.
The prose should make the immediate action, motive, and causal change paraphrasable. The four image
directions must leave real narrative work to the plate rather than describing an illustration of
the prose. Clef remains open at world scope. If a book realizes it, keep that occurrence consistent
inside that book without making it universal.

## Root book: Shape of Time

This proof carries Jay and Tan from the failed payment through Jay's informed yes in eight short
folios. It should feel like a finite movement of a continuing novel, not a synopsis. Let individual
moments occupy the page. If the opening realizes Clef materially, preserve that occurrence through
the rest of this root run.

### 1. Payment — split layout, with a plate

Begin inside Jay's competent late shift at the Oakland shop. Visitors from other Primas coordinates
are ordinary customers whose money matters to the business; the reader does not need a lecture
about them. Tan tries to pay for Clef through Jay's phone, waits for an interface action the phone
cannot perform, becomes embarrassed, and suspects he changed something. Jay sees the mismatch and
gives her the Clef because he does not want the queue watching her fail and because he is curious
about her. End on the gift making less sense to Tan than the failed payment.

The plate should establish Jay and Tan as recurring people and reveal the shop's tourist-facing
refurbishment, accumulated working wear, queue geometry, and unequal attention through hands,
glances, objects, and distance. It must not turn either character into a glamorous science-fiction
type.

### 2. The gift — text-led

Tan returns prepared to make a physical payment and apologizes specifically for assuming Jay was
tricking her. She tries to pay for the earlier Clef; Jay accepts payment only for the new purchase
because the first one was a gift. They exchange names and choose to keep talking. Carry them through
closing to the relocated bus stop: Tan's guide calls it original, while Jay can point to the old
anchor bolts and the coach bay that displaced it. She listens instead of asking him to perform
authenticity. End with her asking when he works next.

### 3. The band — image-led

Jay and Tan have stopped pretending their meetings are accidental. At a local show, Tan knows the
next bar of a song from a compilation sold at her home coordinate, credited only “performer
unknown.” Jay asks whether the musicians in front of them share in what the recording earns. Tan
does not know and does not protect herself with an excuse. Keep the pleasure of the night and their
growing physical ease present alongside the question.

The plate should preserve Jay and Tan from Payment, hold them within the crowd and pleasure of the
performance, and alone reveal a visitor making a recording at the room's edge. The recording should
be a plausible ordinary act, not a glowing temporal effect.

### 4. Your tomorrow or mine — text-led

After the show they admit they have been arranging their lives to meet and they kiss. Tan says she
must leave and will be back Thursday. Jay asks which Thursday and makes her name the Oakland date
and the duration she expects to experience before returning. The question is practical, affectionate,
and necessary. Travel takes work and time; neither destination pauses. End with a promise that
belongs to both calendars.

### 5. The venue — text-led

Tan returns on the agreed Oakland date, physically tired. Later she takes Jay to a traveler-oriented
venue. The attendant assumes he is her hired local guide. Tan gains his admission by saying he is
with her and initially thinks she has defended him. Outside, Jay explains that the correction still
made his access depend on her and that “with me” can describe several unequal relationships when
only she is allowed to decide. Let the rupture be quiet and specific. Tan hears it; neither knows
whether she will return.

### 6. After closing — text-led

Tan returns without an itinerary or purchase and apologizes without asking Jay to erase what
happened. A leaking cold case gives them ordinary work to do together after closing. They mop,
shift stock, disagree, and laugh. Their courtship survives its first honest rupture. Outside, Tan
plainly invites Jay to the place where she lives. Her father can get an application considered, but
she cannot yet answer who sponsors him, how the crossing works, or how he returns if they separate.
Jay says he will listen when she has facts.

### 7. The map — plate-led, with the prepared aperture

Tan brings a physical temporal map large enough to occupy the shop break table. Jay asks what the
route will feel like, how long it takes for his body, who can change it, and what happens when a
current shifts. Her knowledge is precise and attractive to him. Travel is mapped, physical,
difficult movement through Primas, Phantas, and Mystas, not magic or a jump between a past and a
future. Include the exact sentence “The maps were always becoming wrong.” It must remain an honest
sentence in the prose and the founding aperture for the child.

The plate should preserve Jay, Tan, and the shop while making the three-axis route, revision times,
confidence, changing currents, and unequal access graphically legible. It should distinguish what a
company licenses from observations it does not own. Avoid decorative pseudo-mathematics and generic
future-interface imagery.

### 8. Jay says yes — text-led, movement rest

Answers arrive in separate documents. Tan's father can get the application heard; the company is
the sponsor; a return carrier can issue a passage in Jay's own name with documents he keeps. Jay
still depends on trained travelers and live maps, but the edges of that dependence are visible. He
copies the papers and arranges a bounded absence from work. When Tan returns, he names his conditions
and then his positive reason: he loves her, wants to know the ordinary place that formed her, and
wants their relationship to exist on both people's ground. He says yes during an ordinary shift and
turns to the next customer. Travel has not begun; the root book continues from this changed state.

## Child book: The Map on the Wall

This two-folio proof grows from the phrase “The maps were always becoming wrong,” not from Jay and
Tan's plot.

The child receives the exact founding phrase, its lineage, the complete shared world, and only its
own exposed history as current-book history. It does not receive root plates as story-so-far. Eniola
Akinyemi is a probationary municipal temporal-ferry navigator in Lagos. The ferry makes ordinary
spatial circuits on the lagoon while mirrors, heat, and separate instruments move it toward an
inhabited coordinate along Phantas Minor. Lagoon water and the temporal vector field are not the
same thing. The city's licensed chart access is expiring; crews retain witnessed paper soundings.

### 1. The licensed route — text-led

On the last scheduled run, the licensed route disagrees with field instruments and several recent
paper soundings. The company console orders a return that the local observations indicate is more
dangerous than continuing. Eniola wants to keep her probationary credential, while passengers need
to reach work, pickups, and home. She asks the pilot, mirror technician, and veteran passengers to
state what they observe and records their names. She disables automatic return and follows the
witnessed corrections. Carry the physical, tiring crossing far enough that reaching the outer berth
is the consequence of their work, not a cliffhanger.

### 2. The terminal wall — image-led, movement rest

At the outer berth, the company locks the console and suspends Eniola before the last passenger is
off. She copies the actual trace onto a timetable and then the terminal wall, marking the order to
return, every witnessed correction, and the observation times. Other crews gather and add routes
that do not all agree with hers. The official demand to clear the wall cannot make the knowledge
private again. Eniola has lost the ferry; the map has escaped one owner without becoming settled
truth. The child continues with the problem of how public corrections earn trust.

The plate should center Eniola and the Lagos terminal rather than importing Jay, Tan, or Oakland.
It should reveal the closed console, ferry and lagoon context, several materially incompatible
public traces, their witnesses, and the outer stops that depend on them. The root Map plate may
guide only the idea of mapped evidence crossing between private and public use; it is not an
identity, place, palette, or composition reference for Lagos.

## Ordered image-reference decisions

The application, not Fable, makes these choices. The approved Treatment B comparison may be used as
a medium-only reference while explicitly excluding its incidental person, place, palette, objects,
and composition.

- Payment: Treatment B medium only; no prior narrative plate exists.
- The band: Payment, for Jay and Tan identity and the already established book-local visual world.
- The map: Payment followed by The band, for identity across two views and book-local continuity.
- The terminal wall: Treatment B medium followed by The map, where The map governs only the narrow
  parent-inheritance idea described above. Jay, Tan, Oakland, and the root palette must not transfer.

An illustrated folio's plate must be generated, reviewed, and accepted before Fable writes the next
folio in that same book. Rejected images never enter the interleaved history.
